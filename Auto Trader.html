Business car leasing

Whether it's a runaround for a sole trader or a while fleet of company cars your after, business car leasing could be the perfect solution. Find out why.

Business car leasing could be a good choice if you want to drive the latest vehicles while benefiting from potential tax advantages. Read on to learn how it works. Business leasing, also called business contract hire (BCH), lets you obtain brand-new vehicles for your business and employees without a large upfront purchase cost. It is available to business owners, sole traders, partnerships and limited companies, whether you need one vehicle or an entire fleet.

How does leasing a business car work?

Costs follow the same pattern as a personal lease: money up front for the initial rental and a processing fee, then a set amount each month, though firms registered for VAT may be able to claim that tax back. You decide on the car, the contract length, your expected yearly mileage, the size of the initial rental and any extras such as maintenance cover, and should be confident the business can keep paying even in quieter months. A statutory director has to submit or sign off the credit application, made online or by phone, and the provider may want management accounts or bank statements and could also check the directors' personal credit. With approval in place you pay the fee, sign the order form and contract, take delivery, and give the car back once the term is over.

Is business car leasing right for me?

Choosing to lease instead of buy can bring a number of advantages.

1. Potential tax savings: firms registered for VAT could recover part or all of it, but the gain differs from one business to the next, so check with an accountant.

2. Lower upfront capital requirement: the cost is spread across the term rather than paid at once, leaving cash free for other needs, which matters most when running a fleet.

3. Predictable monthly costs: one fixed payment that covers road tax and any chosen extras, over a known term, keeps budgeting simple.

4. Drive a new car: you get the latest models without buying outright, and once approved for a fresh lease you can switch every few years.

5. No need to sell the car: at the end it is checked for fair wear and tear and handed back, with no resale to arrange.

6. Comprehensive manufacturer's warranty: as the cars are new, full factory cover applies*.

There are also some commitments to weigh up.

1. The car never becomes yours, so it cannot be sold or modified.

2. An initial rental and a processing fee are due when the lease begins.

3. Payments run for the whole agreed term once signed, and leaving early can trigger a termination charge.

4. Damage outside fair wear and tear may be billed, so the car needs looking after.

How do I apply for business car leasing?

Start by browsing deals on Car Leasing on Autotrader, picking business offers rather than personal ones if the vehicle is for your company. Set the term, mileage, initial rental and any extras, make sure the outlay is affordable in every month of the year, then apply online or over the phone.

What happens at the end of a business lease?

You need to get in touch to book a collection, since it is not arranged automatically. The car is inspected inside and out; normal wear is accepted, further damage may be charged, and tyres below the legal tread depth will stop the pickup. Returning it ends the contract, and a key-for-key swap can bring your next car on the same day.

Taken together, business leasing swaps ownership for predictable costs and regular access to new vehicles, so it is worth weighing the benefits and commitments above against how your business uses its cars.
