Gold Breaks Below $4,000 as Oil Surge Fuels Fed Rate Fears

Happy Friday, traders. Welcome to our weekly market wrap, where we take a look back at these last five trading days with a focus on the market news, economic data, and headlines that had the most impact on gold prices and other key correlated assets—and may continue to in the future.

So, what kind of week has it been?

Here's what you need to know:

  1. Gold fell under $4,000/oz late Thursday and kept bouncing back over it on Friday, with gains capped around $4,025.
  2. Oil jumped, with Brent close to $115 and WTI over $100, strengthening bets that rates rise and stay high.
  3. Upbeat jobless claims, a firmer Dollar and a slide in tech shares all weighed on gold.
  4. Coming up: Wednesday's Fed decision, then PCE inflation data and the first Q3 GDP estimate.

So, What Kind of a Week Has It Been?

Gold had a tough second-to-last week of October, drifting lower from Monday through several support levels until it dropped beneath $4,000/oz on Thursday night; by midday Friday buyers had lifted it back over that mark several times, but each bounce stalled before getting much past $4,025.

Oil Surge Reinforces Higher-for-Longer Rate Fears

With almost no economic data on the calendar and Fed officials in their pre-meeting quiet period, gold traded mostly on rate expectations rather than inflation itself. Middle East shipping tensions lifted Brent to a five-month high near $115/barrel and pushed WTI above $100, and markets now see this as a stop along the way rather than the top, positioning for rates that go higher and stay there even if the Fed holds next week.

Jobless Claims and a Tech Selloff Add Pressure

Thursday brought two more setbacks for gold. Weekly jobless claims came in at 194,000 against 200,000 expected, a small miss that still suggested the labor market can handle higher rates, which lifted the Dollar and dulled gold's appeal. Later that day a tech-led stock selloff, with the Nasdaq down 2%, looked like a rush into cash that boosted the Dollar further at gold's expense.

Looking Ahead

Wednesday's FOMC decision, followed by the PCE inflation reading and the first estimate of third-quarter GDP, could shape where gold heads for the rest of 2026.

The author signs off by wishing traders a good weekend and promising another recap next week.
