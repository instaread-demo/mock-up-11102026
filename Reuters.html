Trump says he will hold off on fresh Iran strikes for now

By Steve Holland, Yomna Ehab and Alexander Cornwell

WASHINGTON/CAIRO/ABU DHABI, Nov 9 (Reuters) - US President Donald Trump said on Monday he plans to hold off on new military strikes against Iran for the time being after appeals from countries in the region, including from Qatar's prime minister in White House talks.

Trump said he would also keep the naval blockade in the Strait of Hormuz in place for now. He told reporters the US was well armed but did not expect to need that strength, and said he was acting at the request of Qatar, Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Bahrain, Kuwait and other neighbors.

His comments followed a meeting with Qatari Prime Minister Sheikh Mohammed bin Abdulrahman Al-Thani, a key go-between with Tehran, after last week's US midterm election. Trump has played down the chance of major fighting resuming but has not accepted Iran's truce plan, and senior US and Gulf officials doubt a deal is close. Iranian state media reported a US strike on Qeshm island overnight, while Iran has hit tankers and its Houthi allies have hit Saudi Arabia.

DIPLOMATIC MOVES

Al-Thani, who visited Tehran last week, described his talks with Trump and Vice President JD Vance as positive. Iranian security council chief Mohsen Rezaei said on X the US should accept the seven conditions Iran has set.

Iran's plan would trade an end to the US port blockade, some sanctions relief and a halt to bombing for Tehran lifting its own Hormuz blockade, with nuclear talks later. Secretary of State Marco Rubio called the terms unrealistic, while Iran's Foreign Ministry defended them.

Senior Republicans, including Vance, have blamed election losses on the Iran war, but Rubio and UAE official Anwar Gargash both said a settlement did not appear close. Turkish President Tayyip Erdogan said he told Trump that Ankara opposes a new escalation.

MILITARY ACTION

A provincial official told Fars the Qeshm strikes did not affect vital services, and a senior Iranian source told Reuters they targeted radar, missile launcher and mine-laying sites. The US has not commented.

Maritime agency figures show tanker attacks rose sharply in October. Iran said a tanker hit a mine east of the Strait of Hormuz, and a state TV reporter said 11 tankers had been disabled in 10 days.
