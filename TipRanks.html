U.S. Stocks Rise on Lower Oil Prices; Nvidia Stock Falls While Other Chipmakers Gain

  • Nvidia stock declined as the chipmaker’s board opened an investigation into CEO Jensen Huang’s relationship with an alleged Chinese spy.
  • Inflation readings and quarterly results from AI names CoreWeave, Nebius and Cisco are in focus this week.

U.S. stocks rose on Monday morning as oil prices fell after President Donald Trump told CNN that he was “highly unlikely” to order further strikes on Iran. This eased concerns about potential supply disruptions in the Middle East. At the time of writing, Brent crude futures, the international benchmark, fell 4.73% to $93.67 per barrel, while WTI crude futures were down 4.11% to trade at $87.10 per barrel.

The Nasdaq, S&P 500 and Dow were all up between roughly 1.3% and 1.9%, recovering part of last week's losses, which came on fears that AI infrastructure spending would slow after the Democrats won the midterms.

Nvidia moved the other way: its board is investigating CEO Jensen Huang's relationship with a woman whom U.S. intelligence agencies say is a Chinese spy. Other chipmakers rose, led by AMD after an Oppenheimer upgrade to Buy, with Micron, SanDisk, Marvell and Qualcomm also higher.

Later this week, CPI and PPI inflation data come under scrutiny after a strong jobs report raised the odds of a December 9 rate hike, and earnings from CoreWeave, Nebius and Cisco will test the AI trade.
