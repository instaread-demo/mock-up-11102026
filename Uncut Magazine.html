Peter Asher: Everywhere Man reviewed – a remarkable under-the-radar story of Beatle confidant and superstar producer

The McCartney insider who became the architect of the singer-songwriter era

By Alastair McKay 5th November 2026

Although their single “A World Without Love” was a worldwide hit in 1964, the reputation of Peter and Gordon resonates more strongly in the US, where they topped the charts as part of the British Invasion. About this, and most things, Asher remains unfailingly modest, suggesting that the cultural earthquake was 90 per cent the work of The Beatles, with 10 per cent from everyone else.

Built from Asher’s stage show, the film tracks his gift for landing next to great talent: doing A&R at Apple, where he found James Taylor, and co-founding the Indica Gallery, where Lennon met Yoko Ono.

The Beatles link came through his sister, actress Jane Asher, whom McCartney dated. McCartney lodged in the attic of the family’s Wimpole Street home, and it was there that Asher became the first to hear “I Want To Hold Your Hand”.

Asher tells it all without boasting, even as Beatles tourists line up for photos with him.

Other episodes take in Marianne Faithfull, Twiggy and a Paul Jones session with Jeff Beck and McCartney.

His father’s suicide and his ex-wife’s mental health struggles get only brief mention.

His bigger mark on the early-1970s sound came through Taylor, Carole King and Linda Ronstadt, and Lyle Lovett and Rufus Wainwright close the film with “A World Without Love”.

It is a warm look back, with Asher plainly pleased to be centre stage again.
