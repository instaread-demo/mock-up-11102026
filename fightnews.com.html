Emiliano Vargas: The Next Face Of Boxing

Boxing Bob Newman November 9, 2026 6 minutes read 2

By Miguel Maravilla

Undefeated super lightweight Emiliano “El General” Vargas (18-0, 15 KOs), son of boxing legend Fernando Vargas, is gearing up for his next challenge as he takes on Omar Juarez (21-2, 8 KOs) of Brownsville, Texas, in a 10-round bout. Vargas will face Juarez on Saturday, December 5, at Desert Diamond Arena in Glendale, Arizona, as part of the Benavidez vs. Cieslak undercard, which will be broadcast live on DAZN and Prime Video Pay-Per-View.

Vargas said he wants to finish the year strong, with a big main event expected early next year, but only if he gets past Juarez, a tough opponent he expects to test him. He is targeting a dominant ten-round win or an early stoppage.

A veteran of big cards in Las Vegas, New York, Texas and Mexico, he told Fightnews.com he is excited to share the Glendale bill with David Benavidez and Virgil Ortiz, and Fernando said his son keeps growing as a fighter.

Emiliano is training with Hall of Famer Freddie Roach at the Wild Card gym in Hollywood, with his father also in camp. Fernando said his role is to pass on experience and keep his sons disciplined, calling himself “a father and a coach.”

Juarez has won seven in a row since losing to former champion Rances Barthelemy, most recently a third-round stoppage of Emanuel Lopez. Both Vargases expect him to come ready, and Fernando wants a spectacular win.

Vargas is coming off a fourth-round stoppage of Bryce Mills in June at Barclays Center on the Ennis vs. Zayas undercard. He said this fight means everything, calls himself a contender, and believes a 140-pound title shot will come.

Fernando, a 1996 U.S. Olympian, captured a world title in 1998 at 21 by forcing IBF champion Luis Ramon Campas to quit after seven rounds, then beat Raul Marquez, Winky Wright and Ike Quartey. In December 2000 he lost a unification fight to WBA champion Felix Trinidad by 12th-round stoppage after scoring a sixth-round knockdown, a bout many think came too soon.

Asked about regrets, he said champions want to face the best. He has guided Emiliano since he turned pro with Bob Arum’s Top Rank and says he trusts the promoter to decide when his son is ready.

Emiliano also appeared in Bad Bunny’s Super Bowl halftime show this year, and Fernando predicts his son will be the next face of boxing within two years.
